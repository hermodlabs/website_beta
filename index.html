The problem

An abnormal result is visible before its cause is diagnosable.

A result does not tell an engineer what happened or what to do next.

The same apparent failure may come from the unit, the reference, the test configuration, stale data, timing error, measurement uncertainty, or an integration problem several handoffs away.

These explanations matter because they lead to different actions. Before releasing, retesting, reworking, escalating, or intervening, the organization must determine which explanation the available evidence actually supports.

Hermod turns the ambiguous result into a structured case that can be investigated, qualified, and acted on.

How we choose the work

We begin with the decision, not the technology.

Before selecting tomography, machine learning, sheaf theory, a sensor network, or any other advanced technique, we identify what must be found, why it is hard to observe directly, and who must act on the answer.

We then state the credible alternative explanations, determine what evidence would distinguish them, and choose the simplest capability that can support the required decision.

We identify the Joker before deciding whether mapping Gotham is necessary.
