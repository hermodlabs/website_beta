Core judgment

Facts do not contain decisions.

A fact can be correct and still be incomplete. It tells us what was observed. It does not tell us what matters most, who should act, or whether action is worth the cost.

The same reported result

Drone 027 measured 3.1 dB below the reference.

ONE READING

Decision A — hold and investigate

The drone is awaiting customer release, and a short check can show whether the weak result came from the drone or the test system.

The drone should not be released until the measurement is tied to the correct flight, equipment, and test conditions.

Decision B — retest and continue

The reading came from a development flight near known radio interference, and a controlled repeat test is easy to perform.

The same number does not yet qualify the drone as faulty because the environment may explain the weak signal.

The reading did not decide the case. The supporting evidence and the decision at stake did.

Why focused tools

One sensor score should not quietly decide truth, money, workload, authority, and accountability.

Choose what matters most because different priorities lead to different decisions.

A weak signal does not qualify the drone as faulty. First, the evidence must show whether the problem came from the drone, the test setup, or the environment. Only then can the right action be approved.

The unsafe shortcut

Collapse everything into one score.

The score looks simple, but it hides whose priorities were used, which costs were ignored, and why the action changed.

The Hermod approach

Keep facts, judgments, rules, and outcomes separate.

Each user sees the smallest focused tool that completes the job without forcing everyone through every analysis.

Simplicity means showing the right judgment to the right person at the right time.

A sellable value ladder

Start with one recurring drone decision. Add more only when the next problem appears.

Suppose a drone reports a weak radio signal during a test. One customer may only need help proving whether the reading is trustworthy. Another may also need help deciding whether to continue flying, collect more evidence, ground the drone, or verify that the repair was completed.

BASE

Observation and qualification

Did the drone really have a weak signal, and what caused it?

Hermod checks whether the reading came from the correct flight and whether the likely cause was the drone, the ground equipment, interference, or a bad test. A weak reading alone does not prove the drone is faulty.

01

Strategic value

Project Qualification

If weak-signal cases happen once a year and are already resolved in minutes, there may be no product opportunity. If they repeatedly ground aircraft, delay tests, and require senior engineers, the problem may be worth pursuing.

Pursue · Investigate · Hold · Prune

02

Business and operational value

Decision Model

The evidence may show a real signal problem, but the response still depends on the situation. A training flight may be stopped immediately. A drone returning safely from an urgent mission may be allowed to finish before it is inspected.

Continue · Land · Retest · Hold

03

Value of more certainty

Evidence Selection

Before ordering an expensive teardown, Hermod may recommend a ten-minute antenna swap or a repeat test away from interference. If that inexpensive check can settle the decision, more testing is unnecessary.

Check · Defer · Stop

04

Human and governance value

Incentive and Authorization Policy

The operator can report the weak signal without being blamed for causing it. A supervisor decides whether the drone may keep flying, while maintenance must provide evidence before marking the problem resolved.

Report · Approve · Escalate · Verify

05

Verification and learning value

Execution and Compliance Record

Hermod records which drone was grounded, which antenna was replaced, who approved the work, whether the required retest passed, and whether the same problem returns on later flights.

Authorize · Repair · Retest · Learn
